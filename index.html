Gunma Prefecture · ~3hrs from Tokyo
Kusatsu Onsen
Japan's most celebrated hot spring resort town, high in the Gunma mountains at 1,200m altitude. The Yubatake (hot water field) at the town centre is a spectacular sight — a wooden grid framework through which 32,000 litres of near-boiling sulphuric spring water flows every minute, steaming dramatically in the cold January air. The free public baths (Otaki-no-yu, Goza-no-yu) are excellent. The Netsunoyu bath ceremony involves attendants stirring the scalding water with wooden paddles. In January the town is snow-covered and extremely atmospheric. Best as an overnight stay but doable as a very long day — direct JR bus from Shinjuku takes 2hr 50min each way.
Nature Free Public Baths ¥600–800 Walk-in ~3hrs from Tokyo Overnight Snow in January

Yamanashi Prefecture · ~2hr away
Mount Fuji (Kawaguchiko)
January is one of the best months for Fuji views — the mountain is fully snow-capped, the skies are at their clearest, and the crowds are at their annual low. The climbing trails are closed in winter (open only July–early September), but the Fuji Five Lakes area (Kawaguchiko is the most accessible) offers exceptional views. Key spots: Chureito Pagoda with Fuji behind it (the iconic postcard shot), Lake Kawaguchi shoreline, Kachi Kachi Mountain Ropeway (year-round) for aerial views, and the Fuji Visitor Center. Direct Fuji Express buses from Shinjuku Bus Terminal take about 1hr 45min and run frequently. One of the most vlog-worthy days of the entire trip.

Nagano Prefecture · ~3hrs from Tokyo or Kyoto
Nakasendo Trail (Magome–Tsumago)
The Nakasendo was one of the five major roads of Edo-period Japan, connecting Tokyo (Edo) and Kyoto through the mountains. The best-preserved 8km section runs between the post towns of Magome and Tsumago in the Kiso Valley, Nagano Prefecture. Walking this trail in January means a snow-dusted mountain forest path lined with cedar trees, with near-zero other hikers. Magome and Tsumago themselves are exquisitely preserved Edo-period post towns — wooden inns, stone streets, and no modern signage visible — that look identical to how they did 300 years ago. The hike takes 2–3 hours at a moderate pace. Reach Nagiso Station by Shinkansen to Nagoya then the Chuo Line limited express. Can be done from Tokyo or from Kyoto/Osaka as a midpoint stop. Patrick would get extraordinary vlog footage — this is unlike anything else on the itinerary.
Nature Free Trail ~¥5k transport Walk-in ~3hr day trip Full Day Snow in January

Gunma Prefecture
Kusatsu Onsen
Nature Free Public Baths ¥600–800 Walk-in Full Day ~3hrs from Tokyo Snow in January

Japan's most celebrated onsen resort town, situated at 1,200m in the Gunma mountains. The centrepiece is the Yubatake — a wooden grid at the town centre through which 32,000 litres of near-boiling sulphuric spring water flows every minute, steaming dramatically in the winter cold. It is one of the most striking natural sights in all of Japan. Multiple free public foot baths surround the Yubatake; the Netsunoyu bath ceremony involves town attendants stirring the scalding water with wooden paddles to cool it.

In January the town is deep in snow and the contrast of the steaming hot spring against the white landscape is extraordinary — vlog gold for Patrick. The direct JR bus from Shinjuku Expressway Bus Terminal takes 2hr 50min each way and runs frequently. An overnight stay at a ryokan (traditional inn with meals included) is the ideal way to experience it, though a very early start makes a single day possible.

Nagano Prefecture
Nakasendo Trail
Nature Free Trail ~¥5,000 transport Walk-in Full Day ~3hr day trip Snow in January

The Nakasendo was one of the five great Edo-period highways connecting Tokyo (Edo) and Kyoto through the central mountains. The 8km section between the post towns of Magome and Tsumago in the Kiso Valley, Nagano Prefecture, is the best-preserved stretch and can be walked in 2–3 hours. The trail passes through cedar forests, stone-paved mountain paths, waterfalls, and traditional farmhouses — all unchanged from the Edo period.

Magome and Tsumago themselves are exquisitely preserved post towns where no modern signage, vending machines, or telegraph poles are visible — they look exactly as they did 300 years ago. In January the trail is snow-dusted and virtually empty. This is unlike anything else on the itinerary and produces the most cinematic vlog footage of the trip. Reach Nagiso Station by Shinkansen to Nagoya (~1hr 45min) then Chuo Line limited express (~45min). Works either from Tokyo or as a stop between Kyoto and Tokyo. A taxi or bus connects Magome and Tsumago for the return.

Booking Window

Book flights August–September 2026 — the 4–6 month window before departure is historically the sweet spot for transatlantic-plus-transpacific routes. Getting Around

JR Pass — Skip It

The JR Pass was good value before the 2023 price hike. For this trip's routing (Tokyo→Kyoto→Osaka), individual Shinkansen tickets cost approximately ¥14,750–15,270/person total, while the 14-day JR Pass now costs ¥50,000/person. Unless the group plans significant additional JR travel (day trips to Hiroshima, Nikko, etc.), individual tickets are substantially cheaper.

Shinkansen Routes

Tokyo → Kyoto: Nozomi (fastest, ~2h15m) from Tokyo or Shinagawa Station — ~¥14,170/person. Book via SmartEX app or at JR ticket windows.

Kyoto → Osaka: Local JR train (Biwako/Kyoto Line, ~30min) — ~¥580/person. Or Shinkansen (~15min) ~¥1,490/person. Take the local train — it's fine and much cheaper.

Book Shinkansen: SmartEX app (requires foreign credit card registration) from October–November 2026. Seats open 1 month before departure — book on Jan 15 opening for your specific date.

Osaka: The ICOCA card is Osaka's equivalent but Suica works everywhere — no need to switch.

Packing for January

January Weather Overview

January in Japan is cold and dry. Tokyo sits around 5–10°C (41–50°F), Kyoto is colder at 3–9°C (37–48°F), and Osaka is mildest at 6–11°C (43–52°F). Fushimi Inari at sunrise (Day 9) will be near freezing. Rain is uncommon in January but possible in Osaka. Snow is rare in Tokyo and Osaka but Kyoto gets occasional light frost.

Most of the trip involves walking, some hiking (Fushimi Inari, Arashiyama), and time in heated indoor spaces. The temperature difference between outside and indoors can be significant — layering is essential.
